Brisbane pixel diorama

Every building within 5 km of West End, from the CBD towers to the Queenslanders, on the real hills, with live CityCats, trains, buses and planes, cars and cyclists at real traffic levels, and the real sun and weather.

What you're looking at

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, and hover or click a building to see its height and floors. The sun slider moves the shadows from 6am to 5:15pm, and the photo view frames Eagle Street.

The CityCats, ferries, trains, buses and Metro sit at their real GPS positions, and the planes are the real flights passing over Brisbane, so hover any of them to see where it's headed. Each aircraft is drawn as its own type, from Cessnas and rescue helicopters to Dash 8s and A350s. The cars are simulated, but how many are out follows the council's live traffic signal data, and the cyclists ride the bikeways in the numbers its bike counters record.

The Roll button turns the map into a Katamari-style game, where a sticky ball rolls up everything smaller than itself, from cane toads, ibis and kangaroos to CityCats and the Brisbane Skytower, with five levels, a collection book and a best-times board.

In daylight the sun and the weather follow the real time in Brisbane, so the shadows and the clouds are today's.

Buildings and hills are drawn 1.6 times taller than they really are, so the skyline still reads from a distance.

How it was made

The model is one data file: 57,008 building footprints with heights, the trees, 14,304 road segments, the parks and the river, all clipped to a circle 5 km around West End. three.js draws it in WebGL, and the pixel finish, labels and traffic can each be switched off.

The ground follows Queensland's bare-earth LiDAR elevation model, sampled every 10 m, so Mt Coot-tha and the inner-city hills rise out of the map. The river bridges, the tallest towers and landmarks such as the Wheel of Brisbane, the Brisbane sign and the Gabba are hand-built models drawn from published dimensions and photos.

The railway comes from OpenStreetMap, and each train follows the track between its GPS fixes, through the junctions, over the bridges and into the tunnels.

The live ferries, trains, buses and planes come through a small relay on Cloudflare, which fetches each feed every few seconds and shares it with everyone who has the map open.

It was built with Claude in October 2026.

The data

Buildings, streets, parks and the railway are © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL) and the Overture Maps Foundation. The elevation is the State of Queensland's QldDem (CC BY-SA), the ferry, train and bus positions are TransLink's open GTFS-RT feed (CC BY 4.0), the traffic signal data and bike counts are Brisbane City Council's (CC BY 4.0), the weather is Open-Meteo's, and the aircraft positions are adsb.lol and adsb.fi open data.

The map files come to 14 MB, so give it a few seconds on a phone.
